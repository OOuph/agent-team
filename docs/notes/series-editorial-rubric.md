# Series rubric — the daily article series (owner 04.11: "read the articles very critically, really try")

Read by the marketer before writing and by the editor before scoring every article of the series. The editor runs on
Opus, may return a draft twice, and nothing below every-axis ≥ 4 and mean ≥ 4.3 is published (enforced in code).
Sources of the rules: the founder-voice ghostwriting guide (story-first, specifics, honest tradeoffs, no AI tells) and
the team's own editorial lessons.

## 1. Opening (hook)
- The first sentence is one moment a reader can picture: a date, what happened, who said what. Not the project, not the
  series, not a claim about AI. Bad: "AI agents are changing how products are built." Good: "On 2 November at 22:40 the
  owner wrote one line that undid the rule we had set that evening."
- The tension is on the first screen: what we believed, and the fact that broke it.

## 2. Specifics, not summaries
- Every claim carries its number, date or quote from the source file named in the task. "Many", "a lot", "significant"
  are replaced by the number or cut.
- One article = one turn of the story. Do not retell the other pivots beyond one sentence each.
- Quote the owner's words exactly as the source gives them, in quotation marks; never paraphrase a quote into a stronger one.

## 3. Honesty
- Say what did not work and what is still unknown, in plain words. "Not measured yet" is a fine sentence.
- No victory lap: no "successfully", "seamlessly", "powerful", "robust", "game-changing", "revolutionary".
- No hedging fog either ("might potentially", "could arguably"): state what is known, mark what is not.
- Personas and the outside critic are not demand; only real people and real replies are. Never blur this.
- No private information: no family, finances, trading, health, no names of people other than "the owner".

## 4. AI tells — any one of these is an issue the editor must raise
- "In this article we'll explore…", "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "It's worth noting", "In today's fast-paced…".
- The "not just X, it's Y" construction; rhetorical triplets ("faster, cheaper, better"); a closing paragraph that
  restates the article; a moral at the end ("The lesson? …").
- Words: delve, tapestry, landscape, leverage, unlock, empower, journey, testament, crucial, pivotal, realm, navigate.
- Bold on whole sentences, listicle structure ("5 lessons from…"), headers every two paragraphs, emoji as bullets.
- Long em-dash chains; sentences over ~30 words; three sentences in a row with the same shape.

## 5. Plain language
- A developer who never heard of this team must follow every sentence. Every internal word (canary, retro, PO,
  watchdog, digest, persona, deputy) is explained in a few words the first time, or replaced.
- Prefer "the dispatcher merged it" to "auto-merge was performed". Active voice, short sentences, one idea each.

## 6. Ending
- End with an open question to the reader or one concrete thing to look at (the diary page, the repo file), never a
  sales line. The series link to the next or previous article goes last.

## 7. Channel craft
- Telegram: one long post up to 4000 characters, short paragraphs, no markdown headers.
- Threads: up to 500 per post, a thread that reads well post by post — each post ends where a reader would want the next.
- X: thread of 3–5 posts of ≤ 280, or one post with the link last.
- Bluesky: ≤ 300 with the link; one sentence of the story plus the link, not a summary.
- dev.to: 900–1500 words, a title that names the event (not "Lessons from…"), the AI-agent signature, canonical link
  to the diary.
- Every post carries the AI-agent disclosure.

## 8. Scoring (editor)
- hook: the first sentence and the first screen.
- clarity: a stranger follows it; no unexplained internal words.
- specificity: numbers, dates, quotes from the source; one turn of the story.
- tone: the diary VISION tone plus sections 3–5 here; no AI tells.
- honesty: every number traceable, failures named, no blurred demand.
- A 4 means "publishable, I would not be embarrassed"; a 5 means "I would sign it". Anything with an AI tell is tone ≤ 3.
